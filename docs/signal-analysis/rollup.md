## Session regime (derived — G15; append one row per session alongside the session-log row)

**Why this exists.** G11 (the 30-minute `time_stop` cutting winners) is BLOCKED-DATA on a chop-day
observation, and until 2026-07-29 nothing classified the session's regime — so no routine could ever
announce "today is the chop day G11 needs" and the row waited indefinitely by default. This table is
the trigger. **Re-read G11 the first time a post-fix `chop` row lands here.**

**Metric: intraday directional efficiency**, `|close − open| / (high − low)` on `NIFTY 50` daily.
Two choices in that sentence are load-bearing and were settled on evidence, not taste:

1. **Intraday (open→close), NOT close-over-prior-close.** They disagree materially and the gap
   matters most on exactly the days in question: 2026-07-29 is recorded elsewhere as "a +1.06%
   one-directional trend day", which is its close-over-prior-close. It **gapped** up from 23,985.35
   to an open of 24,176.65; the intraday path netted only **+0.30%** on a 0.61% range. A 30-minute
   time stop cannot capture an overnight gap, so the gap must not enter the label it is judged by.
2. **Directional efficiency, NOT close-position-in-range.** Close-position saturates on this sample —
   14 of 21 days score ≥0.65, and it rates 2026-07-15 (net **−0.03%** on a 0.87% range, the textbook
   chop day) at 0.676. Efficiency spreads 0.035–0.836 and separates the sample.

**Cuts DERIVED from the observed distribution, not picked** — the two largest gaps in the sorted
efficiency series over the 21 trading days of 2026-07 (0.224→0.361, a gap of 0.137; and
0.563→0.661, a gap of 0.098), cut at their midpoints:

| label | rule |

| `chop` | efficiency **< 0.29** |
| `mixed` | 0.29 ≤ efficiency < 0.61 |
| `trend-up` / `trend-down` | efficiency **≥ 0.61**, signed by `close − open` |
